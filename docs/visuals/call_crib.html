The claimThe field believes "more reasoning effort sometimes makes models score worse" (overthinking). But benchmarks have output walls, walls delete answers mid-thought, graders score deletions as wrong — and high effort writes longer, so it hits walls more. So how much of the "decline" is real, and how much is the wall? Nobody checks. That's the paper.

The testRaise the wall so thinking can finish, and watch the effort penalty. Shrinks → it was the wall (token starvation). Survives → real (completed negative scaling). Plus Chirag's half: the honest way to count what walls do to lengths. Together: before calling a decline "overthinking," prove the model was allowed to finish.

0.453 walkthrough (spoken)

"Eight responses. Three finished on their own — 128, 192, 256. Five were capped — 128, 192, 256, 256, 384. I only care about lengths where something finished. At 128 all eight are going, one finishes: 7/8 get past. At 192 six are left — the finisher and the capped one from 128 are gone — one finishes: 5/6. At 256 four are left, and here's the subtle part: the two capped exactly at 256 still count, they produced 256 tokens without stopping. One finishes: 3/4. Multiply: 0.547 still going, so 0.453 finished by 256. Capped rows sit in denominators while visible, then drop out — never as finishers. Count the at-256 ones the other way and the answer moves by 0.20, which is why the convention must be stated."

Quick answers (8 likely questions)
What was his note solving?
Logs from shared prompt+answer windows record where the wall was, not real lengths — counting from logs undercounts long answers systematically.
Why chop a copy?
Real chopped logs have no answer key. Chop a copy where truth is kept → every method's guess can be graded. Trust is earned before use where truth doesn't exist.
Why no mean/median?
Longest observation capped → curve never reaches zero → tail unknowable (no mean); curve stalls at 0.547, never crosses 0.5 (no median). Fix: restricted mean to a pre-chosen horizon.
Why not KM on accuracy?
Hidden length exists ("at least this long" is information). Hidden answer doesn't — never written. Repair lengths by counting; retest scores with a bigger wall (new draw, not continuation).
Just one buggy endpoint?
Three layers: the 4,096 default was one endpoint (anecdote) · audit: 4 routes, 2 behaviors, one billing 2.75×, none documented (variance) · walls delete answers + graders count deletions wrong + effort hits walls more (universal mechanism).
Roles?
"You: statistician + supervisor — censoring framework, Helpfulness, the formalism's boundary. Me: experimentalist + first author — harness, audit, runs, empirical sections. The paper is the merger: your framework, my intervention, one wall."
Independence in his Helpfulness data?
Questionable — cap = 512 − prompt, so if long prompts pair with long answers, cutting is informative; his KM undershoots truth on both splits, which leans that way. Fix I understand: stratify by prompt length + average. "But I can't derive the conditional estimators — walk me through it?"
"One generation per cell isn't an experiment."
Correct for run-to-run noise, and unclaimed — it buys a narrow audited pass under $27, reported as effect size + interval, never a verdict. If the paper needs replicates: amend + top up (~$56.56), gladly.
